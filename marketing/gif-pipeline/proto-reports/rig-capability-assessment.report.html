Methodology

The well-design envelope is reduced to a required-capability gate (heaviest string × 1.25 design factor for hookload, floored at the publicly-stated super-spec threshold; mud pressure; pad mode; automation and real-time data interoperability). Every candidate rig is screened pass/fail against the gate; survivors are scored on a weighted fit across capability margin, pad mobility, automation/digital, power/emissions and high-temperature readiness, then ranked. Deterministic and explainable — each score traces to a cited spec.

Basis

  • Super-spec floor (Helmerich & Payne FY2025 10-K): AC drive, ≥1,500 HP drawworks, ≥750,000 lb hookload, 7,500 psi mud system, multi-well-pad (walking) capability.
  • Rig-class specs are marketing-grade, not contractual — binding capability is the per-rig IADC daily drilling report.

Verdict

Four of five candidates clear the super-spec gate. The HT-pack FlexRig ranks first because high-temperature readiness was weighted highest — Patterson's APEX XC carries the most raw capability margin (1,000k lb hookload) but ranks third once the geothermal HT weighting is applied. Nabors PACE-X800 is disqualified on one hard gate — pad capability skidding ≠ walking: its X-Y skid system cannot factory-drill the 12-well pads this program needs.

Caveat: Rig-class defaults are marketing-grade, not contractual. This is the screen + weighted score (stages 1–4); KPI baselining (ROP / cost-per-ft / NPT / uptime) and paid rig-level feeds are a separate step.
