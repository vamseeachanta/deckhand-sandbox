7  Statistical Failure-Risk Analysis — DP & Mooring

For a DP2 monohull heavy-lift vessel doing subsea installation, the dominant station-keeping risk is loss of position (LOP) by drive-off or drift-off while a lift is suspended over or near a subsea structure. IMCA annual DP reviews show no single cause dominates every year — thrusters/propulsion, position-reference loss, power/electrical, computer/control and human/procedural error all feature, with human factors likely under-counted. Highest-consequence windows are the splash-zone crossing (dynamic amplification, slack-snatch, snag) and seabed landing (low excursion margin). On an 8-point mooring, the controlling risks are fatigue/corrosion at terminations/connectors and single-line overload leading to progressive failure; HSE RR444 found roughly one FPSO mooring-line failure per ~5–9 operating-years. BSEE Gulf of Mexico data reinforces that worst real-world outcomes are dropped objects and vessel/structure contact. Net design driver: tolerance to a single station-keeping fault while a heavy load is in the water.

DP2 vs DP3 — why it matters here. DP2 = single-fault tolerant for active components (thrusters, generators, control computers, references are redundant) so no single failure causes LOP. DP3 adds tolerance to a single failure including fire or flood in any one compartment (A60/watertight separation of redundant equipment). For subsea installation, on a DP2 unit a common-mode event (fire, flood, busbar fault, or a worst-case failure that defeats the redundancy concept) can still cause LOP with a load in the water — hence critical lifts are gated on a current FMEA, proven WCFDI, DP trials, and a defined go/no-go envelope. DP3 buys compartment-level survivability that matters most for long-duration / high-consequence ops; for short, well-planned construction lifts DP2 is industry-standard provided redundancy is verified and weather/escape windows are respected (IMCA M 103 / M 117).

Headline. The single Critical rating is DP loss-of-position during a suspended splash-zone lift — a powered/drifting excursion that drags the load into the hull or an adjacent structure. It is controlled by a verified DP FMEA, a pre-lift reference check, a defined abort/lay-down plan and a hard Hs go/no-go gate (§3, §6). Mooring failures are lower-likelihood but the cascade case is high-consequence; design for intact + one-line-damaged (DNV-OS-E301).
